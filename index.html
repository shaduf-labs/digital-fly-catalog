Fruit fly brain simulation projects: what you can try, what is real, and how to build your own

A fruit fly brain now fits in a computer. Scientists mapped the wiring of every neuron in an adult fly brain (the connectome), and people quickly started to run it in games, browser demos and robots. This catalogue lists those projects, checks their links and dates, and says plainly which parts come from the real fly and which parts are ordinary code.

  • Last verification pass: 12 Oct 2026
  • Entries: 140
  • Videos: 56
  • Research reports: 16

Short answer

People have built games, browser demos, desktop pets, art, brain engines and research tests on top of real fly connectomes: mostly the FlyWire brain (2024) and the MaleCNS brain and nerve cord (2026). We checked 140 of them. You can open 47 in a browser or a free notebook today, without installing anything, and in our own browser checks 9 of 12 demos ran on a computer without a GPU.

Most viral demos really do run a simulated fly brain, but a person chose which neurons see the game and which neurons press the buttons, and in several demos scripts or trained parts make most of the movement. No fly has been "uploaded", and several viral claims publish no code at all, among them Pokémon on a Raspberry Pi, the most-viewed, a fly said to play Gorilla Tag, and a fly brain model said to "learn to hack". Is it a brain or just a map? It is a map of one fly's wiring, run with a simple neuron model, and in our own test the map's wiring made a simulated fly turn away from a looming shadow, where a scrambled map did not, and scrambled maps turned up to the same activity still produced no turn command (one model, small n, our own mapping). Our planned wiring test on a trained fly vision model (flyvis) has run out of memory twice (10 and 12 Oct), so it has no result yet; on 9 Oct we read the relay neurons by firing rate and listed what the map leaves out, with one number and one source per part (below). Does the real wiring help in general? Sometimes: across 53 control studies it beat scrambled wiring in 21 of the 44 that test it, mostly reflex, sensory and steering circuits, and tied or lost in trained, reservoir and machine-learning tasks. Our own pre-registered tests show how much the answer depends on how the wiring is scrambled. Even where it helps, a brainless baseline often matches the fly: in Doom a brainless autopilot plays about as well as the full fly brain (the doom-fly-control by gabrycina), and in our own test a simulated fly body walked as far with no brain as with the real one.

To build your own, start with the published whole-brain model on a CPU: in our test on 2 CPUs (Linux, 28 minutes), sugar neurons drove the feeding motor neuron to 85.2 Hz, and shuffled wiring dropped it to 0 Hz. We re-tested the beginner path on 5 Oct 2026 (6 of 6 tests, 82.0 Hz, bit-exact with our earlier runs). The model needs two data files, 104.1 MB (what you download), and the guide has two tested deeper steps: connecting the brain to a walking fly body and adding a sense: a looming shadow; since 8 Oct the brain and the body also install together in one Python 3.12 environment (1.1 GB, numpy pinned to 2.3.5). Teaching? Use in class is a 20-minute plan.

13 Oct: the relays against an activity-matched control

Our own test · pre-registered before the first trial (13 Oct 2026, 10:18 UTC) · brain only · one model · not a real fly

The pre-registered sentence, chosen by the rule fixed in advance:

The two sides disagree: on one side silencing the relays lowered the turn neuron's rate more than matched activity elsewhere, on the other it did not. We do not draw a conclusion.

What is new. On 9 Oct we silenced the relay neurons between the looming-sensitive cells and the turn neuron (DNa02) and compared that with silencing randomly picked active neurons. That random set removed 2-6 times as much activity as the relays (2.3 times for a shadow on the left eye, 5.8 times on the right), so the two were not like for like. This time each silenced relay neuron got its own partner outside the relays that fired about as often in the same trial (within 20% of its spike count), and the control silences those partners instead. 12 Oct: our flyvis motion test, second attempt: still no result

Our own test · pre-registered · stopped for memory · no chart and no result

The question: does the real wiring layout help a trained fly vision model see which way things move? We shuffle the layout in flyvis, a trained model of the fly's motion-vision circuit, and check whether its motion-sensitive cells still prefer the right directions. After the first attempt on 10 Oct, we tried again with a lower-memory setup.

Not decided: a second, lower-memory attempt on 12 Oct also needed more memory than our shared machine has (about 3 GB at its peak; the machine allows 3 GB for everything running on it).

Measured: it ran out while preparing the moving-edge test input, before the first simulation step (sampled peak about 3.17 GB of the 3.22 GB limit, which is 3 GiB). So there is no result in either direction, no ledger row and no chart. We did not retrain the model after shuffling. How to read our evidence grades

Every project gets one grade. The grade says how well the project's own claim about a fly-brain-driven behaviour is supported. It is not a score for quality or fun: a careful study that found a negative result can be grade A, and a great game can be grade D.

  • A Tested against real-fly data or a control
  • B Real connectome runs, inputs and outputs chosen by hand
  • C Partial, stand-in or decorative network
  • D Scripted or ordinary game logic
  • U Unverified claim
  • n/a Dataset or tool, not graded

Read the full grade rules and the viral-claims verdicts.

Two dates on every entry

Last upstream activity

The date of the project's last commit or release on GitHub or its own site. It tells you whether the authors still work on it. Some other lists show the date a repository was created instead, which can be years out of date.

Last verified by us

The date we last opened the link and checked the facts in the entry. A recent date here means the link worked and the licence and setup notes were correct on that day.
