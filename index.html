Fruit fly brain simulation projects: what you can try, what is real, and how to build your own

A fruit fly brain now fits in a computer. Scientists mapped the wiring of every neuron in an adult fly brain (the connectome), and people quickly started to run it in games, browser demos and robots. This catalogue lists those projects, checks their links and dates, and says plainly which parts come from the real fly and which parts are ordinary code.

  • Last verification pass: 29 Sep 2026
  • Entries: 82
  • Videos: 47
  • Research reports: 4

Short answer

People have built games, browser demos, desktop pets, art, brain engines and research tests on top of real fly connectomes: mostly the FlyWire brain (2024) and the MaleCNS brain and nerve cord (2026). We checked 82 of them. You can open 31 in a browser or a free notebook today, without installing anything.

Most viral demos really do run a simulated fly brain, but a person chose which neurons see the game and which neurons press the buttons, and in several demos scripts or trained parts make most of the movement. No fly has been "uploaded", and the newest viral claims (Pokémon on a Raspberry Pi, Rainbow Six Siege) publish no code at all. Where studies test shuffled wiring, the real wiring matters for fly reflexes and for steering a simulated body, but it has not helped general machine-learning tasks, and in Doom a brainless autopilot plays about as well as the full fly brain.

To build your own, start with the published whole-brain model on a CPU: in our test on 2 CPUs (Linux, 28 minutes), sugar neurons drove the feeding motor neuron to 85.2 Hz, and shuffled wiring dropped it to 0 Hz.

This week · 29 Sep 2026

Two viral fly-brain claims, refereed. Both reached the news on 28 September. Neither has published any code, so both are graded U, unverified.

"A fly's brain on a Raspberry Pi 5 plays Pokémon"

Twitch stream · XDA Developers, 28 Sep

A full FlyWire brain may well run on the Pi. But by the creator's own account it is fed positions read from the game's memory, the button decoder and helper scripts are unpublished, and chat steers its learning, so the stream cannot show how much of the progress comes from the fly. Two open-source "fly plays Pokémon" projects (both grade C) are not the stream's code. Our verdict

"A fly's digital brain plays Rainbow Six Siege"

dev.ua, 28 Sep, from an X post

Unverified, and unlikely to be a fly brain at work. Nothing is published, the stated "over 1,000,000 neurons" is about six times the largest fly brain map (166,700), and a model trained on 20 TB of human gameplay would owe its skill to that training. Our verdict

  • The week's strongest evidence is a new grade A control study: a whole fly brain steers a simulated body to targets, even after two legs are cut, and scrambled wiring fails. It is the positive counterpoint to the Doom test, where a brainless autopilot matched the fly.
  • DesktopFly, the most-starred demo, is re-graded from B to C: its neurons mostly switch coded animations. New project page, with a warning about fake download copies.

How to read our evidence grades

Every project gets one grade. The grade says how well the project's own claim about a fly-brain-driven behaviour is supported. It is not a score for quality or fun: a careful study that found a negative result can be grade A, and a great game can be grade D.

  • A Tested against real-fly data or a control
  • B Real connectome runs, inputs and outputs chosen by hand
  • C Partial, stand-in or decorative network
  • D Scripted or ordinary game logic
  • U Unverified claim
  • n/a Dataset or tool, not graded

Read the full grade rules and the viral-claims verdicts.

Two dates on every entry

Last upstream activity

The date of the project's last commit or release on GitHub or its own site. It tells you whether the authors still work on it. Some other lists show the date a repository was created instead, which can be years out of date.

Last verified by us

The date we last opened the link and checked the facts in the entry. A recent date here means the link worked and the licence and setup notes were correct on that day.
