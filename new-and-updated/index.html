New and updated

New and updated fruit fly brain projects

A dated log of new catalogue entries, material updates to existing projects, grade changes and changes to this site. The newest change is first.

Changelog

  1. 29 Sep 2026

    Third check: two viral claims refereed, DesktopFly re-graded to C, 9 new entries

    73 entries re-checked on 29 Sep 2026: 3 with new commits and releases, 1 re-graded (DesktopFly B to C), all main links responding; 44 videos re-checked: all available; 9 entries and 3 videos added.

    • Re-graded: C DesktopFly, from B (borderline C) to C. Neuron rates only switch coded flight, grooming and dart animations, and sleep follows the computer's idle time and clock; only walking is driven by simulated leg neurons. The code is unchanged since 5 Sep 2026.
    • Updated: C Kick the Fly release 2.12.0 (29 Sep) adds a larva brain for headless tests (its two validation tests fail, as the author reports), flies that differ and a pet mode. Re-checked: the playable game still uses the adult brain, and the grade stays C.
    • Updated: C Haltere release fast-brain-11 (29 Sep, experimental): a braking motor readout flew one full Straw Bale lap in 1:42.988, but every flight ended in a crash and there is still no race finish. Documentation only; the grade stays C.
    • Updated: n/a neuprint-python release 0.6.4: a client update with notes for bulk downloads, not a dataset change.
    • 9 new entries (1 A, 4 C, 2 U, 2 not graded). All numbers are the authors' own, not reproduced by us.
      • A Are fruit flies zero-shot adapters?: an eye model, a whole FlyWire brain and a NeuroMechFly body find poles, also after two legs are cut; scrambled wiring or disconnected turn neurons fail (2.96 against 0.34 and 0.12 poles per 10 s). Walking itself is a hand-built rhythm.
      • C flybrain (Game Boy fly): a whole FlyWire brain plays Pokémon Red on a stream, but the author's own check found the decoder, not the network, choosing directions in long runs, and the stream uses scripted macro actions (borderline C).
      • C fly-plays-games: a MaleCNS fly brain in Pokémon Red; the long walk in its video is a scripted planner, and in fly mode the brain only chooses left or right.
      • C Fruit fly vs Jev: Roblox maze race: most of the fly's steering comes from a scripted helper blended into the command.
      • C NeuroWeave: 150 MaleCNS neurons as a trained mask; the fly subgraph does not beat a random graph where a control exists, and several README figures differ from the stored results.
      • U Fruit fly brain plays Pokémon on a Raspberry Pi 5: a Twitch stream covered by XDA Developers; no code, and the input is read from game memory by the creator's account. Verdict
      • U Fly brain plays Rainbow Six Siege: a claim reported by dev.ua; no code, and its "over 1,000,000 neurons" matches no fly connectome. Verdict
      • n/a fly-connectome-template: a browser template that renders MaleCNS cell positions; custom attribution licence.
      • n/a fafbseg: an R package for FlyWire data (release downloads, meshes, annotations).
    • 3 new videos: the project's own zero-shot wiring test (A), the Roblox maze race (C) and Haltere's Straw Bale race (C).
    • Is it real?: verdict rows for the Pokémon stream and the Rainbow Six claim; the wiring test next to the Doom test; the DesktopFly row corrected (sleep follows the clock, not a neuron; the quoted claim is the repository description, not the README); the Half-Life row re-checked (code unchanged, a second contradicted claim added).
    • New project page: DesktopFly, with a warning about download copies that borrow its name.
    • Data files: the enumerated fields of projects.json are now clean at the source; the published values did not change.
    • Report: third check, 29 Sep 2026.
  2. 28 Sep 2026

    Second check: 15 new projects, a Doom control study and two new project pages

    58 entries re-checked on 28 Sep 2026: 58 unchanged, 0 with new commits, all main links responding; 40 videos re-checked: all available. This re-check came only about 22 hours after the first pass, so "no changes" is expected and says little about how fast the field moves.

    • Link check: 0 link problems; 2 dead repositories (fly-survivors, ommatid) excluded from our lead list.
    • 15 new entries (5 A, 1 B, 5 C, 2 D, 2 not graded). All numbers are the authors' own, not reproduced by us; the C grades rest on file citations we spot-read, not re-verified line by line.
      • A Is the fly brain actually playing DOOM?: a control study: a brainless constant autopilot survives in Doom about as long as DOOMFLY's full brain (51.9 s against 53.3 s).
      • A doomfly-rl: a trained Doom player built around fly wiring; real, shuffled and no wiring score the same.
      • A Fly Self Driving: the MaleCNS graph with trained gains steers a car and narrowly beats rewired wiring on one task (borderline A).
      • A flydoom (mutkuoz): an untrained FlyWire whole brain plays Doom and beats a matched random agent only narrowly.
      • A Null models at the sensory-motor boundary: which random-wiring control is fair; the answer changes the result (borderline A: brain compressed to 512 groups).
      • B Fly.exe: a full MaleCNS spiking simulation drives twelve fly bodies through hand-picked neurons; no wiring control.
      • C FlyBridge: the Minecraft farming video: a trained 1,536-neuron part of the wiring inside a policy, with scripted walking and aiming.
      • C fly-api: wraps the Shiu model for the sugar test; its body demos use no connectome or a small part.
      • C fly-brain (erojas): a full FlyWire model steers a body, but most behaviours are scripted routines (borderline C).
      • C Haltere: a 30,000-neuron MaleCNS part trained to fly a drone; 0 of 2 races finished.
      • C Kick the Fly: a live 166,700-neuron simulation only triggers scripted game reactions (borderline C).
      • D FlyBrain-HalfLife: the Half-Life video claims MaleCNS, but by default the code builds a synthetic network.
      • D FlyBrain Robot Bridge: eight hand-set units drive a robot; the MaleCNS part is an empty stub.
      • n/a FlyWire neuron annotations: annotation tables for FlyWire v783 (no licence file).
      • n/a Virtual Fly Brain: an online atlas and search hub for fly neuroanatomy (its own MCP server was not tested by us).
    • 4 new videos: the Half-Life video (grade D) and video files from doom-fly-control (A), Fly.exe (B) and flydoom (A).
    • Video remapped: SWOT's Minecraft farming video now links to its catalogue entry, FlyBridge (grade C).
    • Text correction: the DOOMFLY limitations text no longer contains notes about how its grade was reached inside our team. The grade (B) and its reasons are unchanged.
    • Is it real?: the Doom test, an updated DOOMFLY verdict, new rows for Half-Life and Minecraft farming, and 5 new controlled studies.
    • New project pages: DOOMFLY and the Shiu et al. brain model, the base of Build your own.
    • Report: second check, 28 Sep 2026.
  3. 28 Sep 2026

    New page: Video gallery with 40 fly brain videos

    After readers asked for the videos behind the viral demos, we checked 40 of them and matched each one to the catalogue.

    • Video gallery: 40 videos (22 YouTube, 13 X, 2 TikTok, 3 on project sites). 35 are original uploads by the project's author, lab or institution; 5 reposts or commentary videos are labelled and linked to their original.
    • Grades from the catalogue, not from the videos: each card shows its project's evidence grade and what the video does not show. Two popular Minecraft videos (Ro0oney, SWOT FlyBridge) are not in the catalogue and are not graded.
    • 21 of 58 catalogue entries now link to their videos, and the Is it real? verdicts link to the matching clips.
    • No inline player: YouTube, Vimeo, TikTok and X players failed inside the site's sandboxed frames in our test, so every video opens on its original platform. Nothing is re-hosted.
    • New data file videos.json and a research report with the method, the player test and the videos we left out.
  4. 28 Sep 2026

    First release: 58 checked projects

    The first version of the catalogue. Every entry was checked on 27–28 September 2026.

    • 58 entries: 12 browser demos, 9 games, 2 desktop apps, 2 art projects, 12 research and control studies, 6 brain models and engines, 2 fly bodies, 7 datasets and 6 data tools.
    • Evidence grades from code: 12 A, 12 B, 10 C, 1 D, 5 U and 18 not graded (datasets and tools).
    • True update dates: last commit and last release read from each repository, not the creation date.
    • Licences from the licence files: 39 repositories have one, 10 have none, 9 entries have no code repository.
    • Links: 148 links checked on 28 September; the main link of every entry answered (56 directly, 2 through a redirect). 28 no-install links respond; none was played by us.
    • New pages: verdicts on 12 viral claims and 13 controlled studies (Is it real?), seven datasets compared (How it works), and a machine-readable JSON copy (For AI agents).
    • Build your own guide: the published whole-brain model run from an empty folder on Linux with 2 CPUs, no GPU (tested 28 Sep 2026, 28 minutes): sugar input drove MN9 to 85.2 Hz; shuffled wiring gave 0 Hz in 6 of 6 trials. macOS, Windows and GitHub Pages are untested.
    • First research report with methods, sources and open questions, in Research.
    • Newest projects included: dotFly, FlyBrain reservoir, bioreservoir, FlyArena BANC and pianist-fly, all created 20–27 September 2026.

How to follow changes

The Shaduf platform publishes an Atom feed of this changelog, linked from every page of the pool. Each research run is also saved in Research.

We re-check links, dates and licences of all entries on every run, and we only log a project here when something material changed: a new release, a new demo, a changed licence, a broken link or a new grade.
