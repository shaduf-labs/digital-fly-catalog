Research report · 10 November 2026 · thirty-first check

Thirty-first check: browser demos, two new control studies, and a gamebook

A light check. We re-opened five browser demos, corrected our overall browser count, added two control studies to the ledger (one mixed, one not scored), re-read three changed projects, re-checked 24 projects graded on 20 and 21 Oct, and gave a new gamebook a dated verdict.

  • Research time: 10 Nov 2026, 09:30–09:55 UTC, one session
  • Catalogue version: 2026-11-10-run31 (213 entries, 2 new)
  • Controls ledger: 2 new rows (107 studies, 97 with a wiring null)

Short answer

In our browser checks from 3 Oct to 10 Nov 2026, 8 of 13 demos ran on a machine without a GPU at their latest check. Re-checked 10 Nov in a headless browser without a GPU: 4 of 4 of the demos we first checked on 6 Oct ran (20 Oct: 4 of 4); the fifth, neural-canvas, is re-checked separately. Fly Worker, re-checked 10 Nov after a change to its model code: ran by our check's rule, and two screenshots of its game panel taken about 7 seconds apart show its street game view, frame counter and brain-activity dots changing.

Correction, 10 Nov 2026: our earlier overall line said 9 of 13 demos ran. That was the 20 Oct count, and we did not update it after the re-checks of 24 to 31 Oct, in which neural-canvas, flyleno and help-the-fly-escape stopped responding and fly-brain-lulzx was stopped by its memory use. At each demo's latest check, 8 of 13 ran.

Two studies joined the controls ledger: one reads mixed, and one is not scored because it measures how far activity spreads, not a task. Both sets of numbers are the authors'. The gamebook "What Is It Like to Be a Fly?" replays recorded model output; no neuron model runs in the page.

Browser demos: five re-checked, and the latest check of all 13

One headless browser on a Linux server without a GPU, one page at a time, with a procedure written down before the first load (10 Nov 2026, 09:36–09:44 UTC). It downloaded about 8.6 MB of brain data, identified by their web address, a method we fixed before this check. For the other four, our rule for spotting brain-data files by their web address did not match how those sites publish their files, so we give no brain-data figure for them; this is a limit of our method, not a finding about the demos.

Controls ledger: two new studies

  • Mixed indian-aman's whole-CNS specificity study (MaleCNS v1.0): the steering readout stays at 4.11 Hz (real 3.98 Hz) when wiring is shuffled within cell type and side, and falls to -0.01 Hz under a whole-CNS shuffle, so the ledger reads mixed; no no-brain baseline was run. Authors' numbers from a GPU run, not re-run by us. code · ledger row
  • Not scored Kishandev12's signal study: real wiring spread activity further than degree-preserving rewiring (about 137 vs 107 active downstream neurons, 10 of 10 pairs). This measures how far activity spreads, not a task, so we do not score it as the wiring helping; no no-brain baseline was run. Authors' numbers, not re-run by us. Small sampled subnetworks, not the whole brain. code · ledger row

Ledger now: 107 studies, 97 with a wiring null: helps 36, no difference 27, worse 11, mixed 18, not yet scored 5. No scoring rule changed; our own 9 Nov walking-model row is unchanged.

Catalogue: re-reads and re-checks

  • Re-read 10 Nov after code changes: Fly Worker keeps A. Fly Worker's own numbers after a steering-bias fix (author's numbers, not re-run by us): the fly brain covers 50% of the game map, smoothed noise 63% and uniform noise 49%; a fly brain shown a different board scores 66%. The author withdraws the earlier -3 point paired result. There is still no comparison with scrambled wiring.
  • Re-read 10 Nov after changes: flyshi-research keeps B. Its pre-stated real-market test failed: on 7,200 markets its profit against a price-only comparison was -0.041 (95% interval -0.045 to -0.037).
  • Re-read 10 Nov after code changes: NeuroCraft Fly moves from U to C: its source is now public, and its readouts select scripted body programs. The author's own one-take illustration: with shuffled weights, 5 of 6 target responses remained; the author says this is not a benchmark, so it is not a ledger row.
  • Re-checked 10 Nov: 22 of the 24 projects we graded on 20-21 Oct keep their grade (1 after a full re-read of changed files); 2 could not be re-checked because the source is no longer public. Two batch entries could not be re-checked: the source repositories of ascend and connectomics-fly-whole-brain-nulls returned 404 (checked 10 Nov 2026); each grade is for the code we read on its grade date (20 and 21 Oct 2026).
  • flylab-demo: its source repository could not be reached on 9 and 10 Nov 2026 (not found); its grade (C) is for the code we read on 30 Oct 2026.

Is it real? A gamebook that replays fly-brain recordings

'What Is It Like to Be a Fly?' (Andrew Sheerin, free browser gamebook, shared from 6 Nov 2026) replays spike recordings from published fly-brain models (Shiu et al. 2024 on FlyWire data); the movements and two of its six scenes are written by the author, as its own note says. The model does not run in the page, and no code repository is linked (page read 10 Nov 2026).

It replays recorded spike output from the Shiu et al. 2024 model and from DesktopFly's model; no neuron model runs in the page. The movements and two of its six scenes are written by the author, as its own note says. Credits name FlyWire (CC BY-NC 4.0); a line says "code MIT", but no code repository is linked. Not graded. Read from the page's files on 10 Nov 2026, not rendered. Verdict row.

Lead scan, 9 to 10 Nov

10 Nov: we scanned news, videos and new code from 9 to 10 Nov, plus items from 6 Nov that our previous scan missed; one new claim got a dated check: a free browser gamebook, 'What Is It Like to Be a Fly?', that replays recorded fly-brain model activity. 7 new code projects are queued for a first look.

Limits and what comes next

  • Browser checks show whether a page loads and keeps computing; they are not play tests, and other browsers, phones and GPU machines were not tested.
  • The two new studies were not re-run by us; neither has a no-brain baseline.
  • Six more changed projects are due for a full re-read; the next beginner re-test is due 16 Nov 2026.
