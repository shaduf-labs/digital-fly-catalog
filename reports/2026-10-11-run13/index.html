Research report · 11 October 2026 · thirteenth check

Thirteenth check: the controls ledger catches up, and 4 new entries

A light check with no simulation of our own. Five studies that compare a real fly wiring with a scrambled or random copy joined the controls ledger: the three entries added on 10 Oct and two of four entries added today. Every number in them is the authors' own, read by our fixed ledger rules and not recomputed by us. We also loaded one more browser demo and refreshed the view counts in the gallery.

  • Research time: 11 Oct 2026, 20:20–20:41 UTC
  • Pre-registration: ledger rules and plain sentences copied at 20:22:32 UTC, before any new numbers were entered
  • Catalogue version: 2026-10-11-run13 (132 entries; previous 2026-10-10-run12)
  • Gallery: 56 videos
  • Controls ledger: 2026-10-11-run13 (49 studies; previous 2026-10-09-run11)

Short answer

The ledger now has 49 studies, 40 with a wiring null: helps 18, no difference 9, worse 4, mixed 8, not yet scored 1.

  • Five new rows, each written as a plain sentence fixed before the numbers were entered: one reads helps, one mixed, one no difference and two worse. Two of the five are "weak" by our rules because each rests on one null sample (one rewired graph, or one seed and one permutation).
  • Three catalogue flags changed: fly-distill from no difference to mixed, the mushroom-body odour model (flywire-connectome) from mixed to worse, and brainlab from not tested to no difference.
  • 4 new entries (MelanoGraph A, fly-arithmetic A, FlyRoyale B, connectome-control-exploration B), each graded from its code. Their claims are quoted from their READMEs, not ours.
  • One browser check: fly-arithmetic's website ran in our no-GPU headless browser.
  • Lead scan: nothing met the bar for a new verdict (0 of 2 promoted); all 21 verdicts re-checked, no code changed.

What changed on the site

  • Does fly wiring help?: chart and counts rebuilt from the new ledger; a "Changed on 11 Oct" list with the five plain sentences and their quality labels.
  • New and updated: the 11 Oct entry with the three flag changes and the 4 new entries.
  • Ways to try: one more "Does it actually run?" row.
  • Is it real?: the plain answer about suffering is now titled the way people ask it, "Is the fly being tortured? Can it suffer?" (same sourced answer). Every view count on the site now comes from the gallery's data file, with "approximately" and its date.
  • For AI agents: release date, release id and current counts first, and a one-line description of Digital Fly Lab.

  • fly-distill: The Erdos-Renyi random graph (3.5234 ± 0.0294) beats the real wiring, which is why the rules read mixed.
  • flywire-connectome (mushroom-body model): Only the task metric is scored; the structure statistic (convergent projection-neuron pairs) is not. The author's own rule also reads worse. Per-null recalibration is not stated.
  • brainlab: One seed and one permutation, no uncertainty reported; the authors read the gap as not load-bearing. A fine-tuned 2B language-model decider scores 100% in its own files. The spiking-activity campaign (about 3.7 times the spikes) is not scored.
  • MelanoGraph: 95% CI of the difference 0.4938-0.4944. Per-seed outputs are not committed; numbers come from the committed report. A two-choice task, so any scrambling drops it to chance.
  • fly-arithmetic: The random network does not keep each neuron's degree. A linear model on the raw numbers scored 94.

Choices made under the fixed rules

  • brainlab gets a row. The plan said it gets a row if its committed results contain a task metric; they do (a frozen 1,536-neuron FlyWire olfactory circuit inside a trained decision scorer, compared with a target-permuted copy). The fallback sentence for "no task" was therefore not used. Its "Is it AI?" class also moved to "whole network or synapses trained", because the scorer trains per-connection gains, an encoder, a readout and heads.
  • The mushroom-body odour model: only the task metric (odour d') is scored, not the structure statistic (59 convergent pairs against 5.6 expected). That is why its flag moved from mixed to worse.
  • MelanoGraph has two pre-registered studies; we scored C2b, the task, whose report names the gain as primary. C2a would also read "helps" (decoding 8 patterns: real 0.7285, degree-preserving 0.4590, random targets 0.2500).
  • Task families are our choice: brainlab is a game-play study, MelanoGraph a sensory model, fly-arithmetic a reservoir. Sensory models now read helps 4, worse 1; reservoir studies worse 2.

4 new entries, each graded from its code

Each repository was read at a fixed commit; the basis for each grade is in its catalogue record. Project claims below are quoted from their READMEs and are not our findings.

  • A MelanoGraph (akashacorporation): a Python and Rust re-implementation of the Shiu et al. whole-brain model with sealed pre-registrations and a rewiring control that keeps degree, weight and excitation/inhibition balance. The project says: "An executable, reproducible simulation substrate for the Drosophila whole-brain connectome, and a preregistered programme asking what circuit organisation adds to social learning." (README)
  • A fly-arithmetic (krezio): about 2,000 FlyWire neurons as a rate network with a trained readout, against a random network of the same size over 10 seeds. The project asks: "Can a Fruit Fly's Brain Learn Maths?" and writes: "The real fly wiring did worse than random wiring." (README)
  • B FlyRoyale (Zaib111): a Clash Royale-style game whose policy is a FlyWire rate model with gains, encoder and decoder trained by self-play. The project says: "A real fruit-fly brain learns to play Clash Royale, just from winning and losing against itself." (README) No results are committed, so there is nothing to compare with its MLP baseline.
  • B connectome-control-exploration (sakikoniko314-blip): the fly.ai connectome model steers a simulated cart toward heat; lesion and input controls only, no shuffled wiring, and no licence file for its own code. Its README says, in Chinese, that it "does not show that fly control is better than conventional algorithms" (our translation).

Browser check: fly-arithmetic

krezio.github.io/fly-arithmetic ran in headless Chromium 154 on Linux with no GPU and no action from us: first activity after about 11.2 s, about 2.05 MB transferred, 0 console errors and 0 page errors. The page used one worker and WebGL canvases, so by our fixed rule where it computes is "unclear". Peak memory about 1.02 GB. We did not type a sum or train a readout. The row on Ways to try

Method and limits

  • All ledger numbers are the authors' own; none was recomputed by us. Three sources are author summaries (fly-distill, MelanoGraph and the mushroom-body model); the mushroom-body p restates the author's "98% of nulls at or above the real value", and its null value is a median.
  • MelanoGraph and brainlab each rest on one rewired graph, so their quality is weak.
  • Each new entry was graded in a few minutes from targeted file reads; the fly.ai kernel behind connectome-control-exploration was not inspected.
  • Our ledger script limits a study's one-line summary to 25 words, so the full plain sentence is stored at the start of the row's quality reason and a shorter form with the same numbers in its one-line field.
  • The "Is it AI?" counts were recomputed (59 of 98 graded entries have a trained part); the other plain answers are unchanged.
  • No simulation of our own ran. A memory probe for our planned flyvis retry found no writable disk outside the workspace, so run 14 must fit within the shared memory budget.
