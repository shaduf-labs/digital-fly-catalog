Research report · 21 October 2026 · twenty-second check

Twenty-second check: the 30 spikes/s matched test with a second input draw, and eight new ledger rows

We repeated our matched test of scrambled brains at 30 spikes/s with a second, independent draw of the same input, on the same four scrambled brains, and walked the one with a small steering signal in the simulated body. We graded nine new catalogue entries (eight with a ledger row), re-checked repositories with new commits, read neural-canvas's code, and caught up on video view counts.

  • Research time: 21 Oct 2026, 09:30–09:57 UTC, one session
  • Pre-registration: brain test stamped 09:31 UTC, before the first trial; body walk stamped before the body install; the public sentences were written in advance
  • Catalogue version: 2026-10-21-run22 (195 entries; previous 2026-10-20-run21)
  • Gallery: 56 videos, all available on 21 Oct 2026
  • Controls ledger: 2026-10-21-run22 (82 studies, 72 with a wiring null; 8 new rows)

Short answer

With a second input draw, the matched scrambled brains again sent no turn command (2 input draws on the same 4 scrambled brains). As at 80 spikes/s, none of the scrambled brains we ran at 30 spikes/s sent a turn command, including those strengthened to about the real brain's activity outside the input.

With this input the real brain's turn signal on the right side was 6 spikes/s against our threshold of 5, so the real brain itself sits close to our turn rule at this stimulus strength.

In the body: "Walked in the body, the matched scrambled brain with a small steering signal changed the fly's heading by about 1.3 degrees away from the shadow compared with no brain input, under our 10-degree turn rule; the real brain, with the same input, turned it away by about 9.2 degrees (one run each)." "With this input the real brain's own turn was also under our 10-degree rule." "This body run turns the brain's firing rates into steering through our own hand-made mapping; it is not a separate test of the wiring."

Fly wiring beat scrambled wiring in 30 of 72 studies. Both new "helps" rows come with their baseline: in the pinball study a no-brain hand-written reflex scores higher than the real wiring (11.480 vs 11.221), and the whole-brain nulls study reports no no-brain baseline (0.5 is chance).

One brain model, one 1-second trial per scrambled brain, one body run; a simulation, not a real fly. Ledger numbers are the authors' own and were not re-run by us.

Our own test · pre-registered before the first body run (14 Oct 2026, 09:28 UTC) · simulated body (FlyGym) driven through our own mapping · one model · a simulation, not an animal

On 8 Oct the brain model's turn command appeared from about 30 spikes per second per looming neuron. On 14 Oct we walked the remaining brain drives of that series in the simulated body, so every drive that differs from the no-brain floor now has a body run. The sentences were chosen by rules fixed in advance; the two eyes read differently, so there is one sentence per eye.

Left eye (pre-registered sentence): In the body, the fly turned away from 30 spikes/s per looming neuron upward on this side, the strength at which the brain's turn command appeared; the turn was about 11 deg at 30 and about 37 deg at 80 (our mapping turns the brain's rates into steering).

Right eye (pre-registered sentence): At 30 spikes/s the turn was borderline: the fly turned away in 1 of 2 runs on this side; from 40 upward it turned away in every run. This borderline rests on one run: seed 0 at 30 spikes/s turned 9.18° beyond the no-brain floor, 0.8° under our 10° rule (seed 2 at 30 turned 16.8°).

These body runs turn the brain's firing rates into steering through our own hand-made mapping, so they show the brain result in degrees; they are not a separate test of the wiring. We ran scrambled brains at 30 and 80 spikes/s, not at 20, 40 or 60.

17 Oct 2026: scrambled brains at 30 spikes/s (pre-registered sentences; brain only, because their steering drives equal the no-brain drive):

17 Oct: at 30 spikes/s, the weakest tested input at which the real brain's turn command appeared, we also ran 2 scrambled brains per side (each neuron keeps its number of connections; one 1-second trial each, with the same trial seed as the real-wiring body runs).

  • Left eye: On the left side, neither scrambled brain sent a turn command, and their steering signals were identical to having no brain, so in the body they walk like the no-brain fly (the real wiring turned it about 11 deg).
  • Right eye: On the right side, neither scrambled brain sent a turn command, and their steering signals were identical to having no brain, so in the body they walk like the no-brain fly (the real wiring turned it about 17 deg).

The scrambled brains were not matched for overall activity: they fired 48% (left) and 44% (right) as many spikes as the real brain at this strength. Most of those spikes were the stimulated input neurons themselves; outside the input, the scrambled brains fired about 1% as many spikes as the real brain.

9 new entries and 8 new ledger rows

Two entries were open leads from 20 Oct; seven come from a list of projects with wiring controls (townie/awesome-fruit-fly), read in their own repositories. One more lead was excluded: it has no result file, and its controls are not a wiring scramble. The seven new A grades rest on the authors' committed results, which we did not re-run; FlyGateDemo's numbers are reported in its README only, with no result file.

  • Helps Drosophila Cadet (pinball) A: a 373-neuron MaleCNS v1.0 looming-escape circuit presses the flippers of 3D Pinball: in the author's committed log the real circuit scores 11.221 against 10.770-10.985 for six degree-preserving shuffles, while a no-brain hand-written reflex scores higher, 11.480 (score per life as log(1 + points), 96 paired lives). Stat note: not re-run by us; a separately trained 444-neuron brain shows no difference (real 11.186, shuffles 11.209-11.311); the author notes the comparison was framed after the fact. Fair, strong; the no-brain baseline is not beaten.
  • Helps Whole-brain nulls (Shiu et al. model) A: a reimplementation of the Shiu et al. whole-brain LIF model on FlyWire v783, scored on 157 of 164 published experimental tests: in the author's committed results the measured wiring scores 0.879 balanced accuracy against null means of 0.501-0.821 (four families, 100 draws each, 0 of 100 reaching it); no no-brain baseline is reported, and 0.5 is chance. Stat note: not re-run by us; two null families leave the network largely silent, and the margin over the cell-type block null is narrow and moves with analysis choices (0.039 to 0.064). Fair, moderate.
  • No difference FlyGateDemo A: a C# rate model of the MaleCNS v1.0 olfactory circuit blocks scanner probes on a blog after one punishment per probe family: by the numbers reported in the README, real wiring blocks 59% of later probes, shuffled wiring 54-59% and random wiring 64-68% (3 seeds each). Stat note: no result file is committed and we did not re-run it; a no-brain baseline, cosine similarity on the 53 receptor channels, catches 62-98% of probes per family. Fair, weak.
  • No difference Fruit Fly Plays Fruit Ninja A: a 4,386-neuron MaleCNS v1.0 circuit with a fitted readout: in the author's committed evaluation, mean reward 24.85 real, 24.80 for a target-permuted circuit with its own readout, and 24.95 for a no-brain pixel rule (20 episodes). Stat note: one rewired graph; not re-run by us. Partly fair, moderate; the no-brain baseline is not beaten.
  • No difference Fly Touhou A: a 600-neuron MaleCNS v1.0 rate circuit with an evolved readout dodges bullets: in the author's committed results it survives 1,386 frames, one degree-preserving rewired circuit trained the same way 1,398, and standing still 886 (20 test seeds). Stat note: the author's p = 0.9351; one rewired circuit; not re-run by us. Partly fair, moderate.
  • No difference The Fly Hears A: spoken digits through a 30,000-neuron MaleCNS v1.0 LIF reservoir with a trained reader: in the author's committed results 70.0% real, 69.7% with one scrambled graph and 66.9% for a reader on audio features alone (3,000 clips, 6 speakers). Stat note: with synthetic noise the scrambled graph is ahead (70.0% vs 68.2%); not re-run by us. Partly fair, weak.
  • No difference Boltzmann-fly A: a Deep Boltzmann Machine world model with couplings masked to the MaleCNS v1.0 mushroom body: in the author's results, visit AUC 0.712 with the fly mask, 0.712 with a degree-preserving rewired mask and 0.713 for a plain MLP. Stat note: one seed for data, mask and training; not re-run by us. Partly fair, weak.
  • No wiring null Fly School B: a MaleCNS v1.0 whole-brain LIF fly with a dopamine-gated mushroom-body rule: in the author's committed results a rewarded odour cuts the chosen output by 45-49% in 8 of 8 simulated flies, a never-paired odour by 1-4%. No scrambled wiring was compared, so the row has no wiring null and is not counted in the 72. Weak.
  • Music in the Body C: a browser music piece that runs the MaleCNS v1.0 wiring as a rate network in a Web Worker and turns its activity into music; a rewired mode exists for listening, but nothing is measured, so it has no ledger row.

The ledger now has 82 studies, 72 with a wiring null: helps 30, no difference 20, worse 6, mixed 13, not yet scored 3. No scoring rule changed. Each new row with its stat note: Does fly wiring help?

Re-checks and tracking

  • Repositories with new commits. flybench was re-graded and stays A (its README claim was corrected and new results were added on the published Shiu et al. graph); NeuroPest was re-graded and stays B (version 0.9.0; still no wiring comparison). fly-racer, help-the-fly-escape and three others changed in ways that do not touch what we graded. Two more repositories changed in substance and will be re-graded in the next run.
  • neural-canvas. The code explains the loading card: the about 77 MB of connectome arrays are fetched, and the Web Worker that runs the simulation starts, only after the visitor presses "Download & start", which our check did not press (code read 21 Oct 2026). The simulation then runs inside that Worker, on WebGPU when it passes a check against the JavaScript version and in JavaScript otherwise. This explains why our 20 Oct check saw only a loading card and about 6 MB.
  • flyproject.io. Our check's rule labelled the compute as in-browser (Web Worker), but its size test counted two 3D model files (about 39 MB) as brain data, so the compute label stays unclear.
  • Kick the Fly. The grade now cites the current Rust files (the Python files it cited moved to a legacy branch); the mechanism is unchanged and the grade stays C.
  • Code links. 14 more entries now link their code repository, where the sources, the grade record and the tracked repository agree.
  • The Odyssey-3 claim (the post): No method, code or write-up was published as of 21 Oct 2026. The verdict row stays U.
  • Views. 10 gallery videos have new view counts, read from YouTube search pages sorted by views on 21 Oct 2026 (approximate). Video ids we found that are not in the gallery stay leads until they are checked.
  • Repositories of 141 catalogue entries checked; of 277 link rows, FlyDoom's repository still answers "not found". All 56 gallery videos available. Package check: only setuptools has a newer release (84.2.1); none of the beginner guide's pins changed.

What we could not resolve

  • The match is on spike count only. The matched scrambled brains fire in 1,256 to 2,741 neurons, against 427 and 446 in the real brain. The closing covers 2 input draws on the same 4 scrambled graphs, at 30 spikes/s only.
  • Two matches sit near the band edges: 0.75 (left, shuffle 4) and 1.29 (right, shuffle 5) of the real count.
  • At this input our body rule does not separate real from scrambled on the right side. The real brain turned the fly about 9.2 degrees, also under our 10-degree rule, against 1.3 for the scrambled brain; only the brain-level turn signal separates them (6 against at most 1 spike/s).
  • New ledger rows. All rest on the authors' committed numbers (README numbers only for FlyGateDemo, whose code is C#); none was re-run by us. Some values are midpoints of reported ranges, computed by us and marked as such.
  • New projects. Our GitHub search found 12 more repositories that we have not checked yet; they are not in the catalogue.
