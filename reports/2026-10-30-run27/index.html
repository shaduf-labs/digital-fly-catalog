Twenty-seventh check: the walking-circuit paper's shuffle test, four new entries and four older demos re-checked

We read the shuffle test in the code of a new Nature paper on a fly walking circuit, graded three more projects that compare real and scrambled wiring, re-checked 27 earlier grades and loaded four older browser demos again.

  • Research time: 30 Oct 2026, 09:24–09:50 UTC, one session
  • Catalogue version: 2026-10-30-run27 (209 entries; previous 2026-10-29-run26)
  • Gallery: 57 videos; 57 of 57 available on 30 Oct 2026; no new view counts
  • Controls ledger: 2026-10-30-run27 (101 studies, 91 with a wiring null; 4 new rows)

Short answer

The walking-circuit paper's shuffle test is about the shape of the wiring, not about walking. The authors' code includes a test that shuffles the wiring while keeping the balance of excitatory and inhibitory connections; their saved notebook output shows the number of three-neuron circuits of the walking-rhythm type among front-leg nerve-cord neurons: 21,544 with the real wiring and 306.95 ± 29.10 (mean ± SD) across 100 shuffles. We did not re-run it (checked 30 Oct 2026). This is a count of circuit patterns in the wiring, not a walking test with shuffled wiring, so our ledger does not score it.

Three more projects compare real and scrambled wiring, and none of them shows a clear advantage for the fly wiring: one no difference and two mixed. Fly wiring still beats scrambled wiring in 35 of 91 studies.

30 Oct: re-checked in the same headless browser without a GPU: 1 of 4 older demos ran (17 Oct: 1 of 4). 27 entries first graded 13-19 Oct were re-checked on 30 Oct: 25 keep their grade; 2 with larger upstream changes are re-read next.

The walking-circuit paper: what its shuffle test measures

Code at commit 10e7661 (MIT licence), the saved output of its Figure 3 notebook and the preprint abstract · read 30 Oct 2026 · nothing re-run

Pugliese, Tuthill, Brunton and colleagues (Nature, 28 Oct 2026) simulated rate models of the fly's ventral nerve cord, built from four wiring maps (MANC, FANC, BANC and the male CNS), and pruned them to a small circuit that still produced leg rhythms in the model: two excitatory neurons in a chain and one inhibitory neuron inhibiting both. The paper also reports tests in real flies, which we did not check.

In the shuffle, each front-leg neuron's outgoing weights are moved to other targets, so each neuron keeps its sign and its outgoing weights; incoming connection counts are not kept. Of the 21,544 circuits in the real wiring, 19,799 were oscillatory in the authors' linear estimate (shuffles: 302.66 ± 28.45). The shuffled wiring is not run as a walking or rhythm simulation in the output we read, so our ledger records the study but does not score it.

Catalogue entry: pugliese-2026-walking-cpg, grade A, peer-reviewed, MIT licence. A nerve-cord study, not a whole-brain or body simulation. Verdict row on Is it real?

What we could not resolve

  • The Pugliese test counts circuit patterns in one wiring map (MANC front leg); whether shuffled wiring would still walk was not tested in the output we read. We could not open the journal page.
  • RiskFly has five seeds (p = 0.0625 is the smallest possible with five paired seeds) and fly-novelty-scada three; neither is re-run by us.
  • The seven closed candidates were judged from search lines and file names only; fly-naf and fly-worker were judged to have material changes from commit messages and file lists only, and are re-checked later.
  • flybrain.app's computing location stays unclear: our browser recorded a call to a server, which we did not inspect.
