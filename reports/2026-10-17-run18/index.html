Research report · 17 October 2026 · eighteenth check

Eighteenth check: scrambled brains at 30 spikes/s, and the browser demos re-checked

Our looming test showed the real brain's turn command from about 30 spikes per second per looming neuron, but until now every scrambled brain we ran saw the strongest input, 80 spikes per second. This time we ran 2 scrambled brains per side at 30. We also re-checked the six browser demos of 3 Oct, added 4 catalogue entries and 2 ledger rows, and counted whether a stricter relay control can be built.

  • Research time: 17 Oct 2026, 23:00–23:35 UTC, one session
  • Pre-registration: reading rules and public sentences stamped at 23:00:25 UTC, before the drive check and the first trial
  • Catalogue version: 2026-10-17-run18 (156 entries; previous 2026-10-15-run17)
  • Gallery: 56 videos, all available on 17 Oct 2026
  • Controls ledger: 2026-10-17-run18 (60 studies, 51 with a wiring null; 2 new rows)

Short answer

17 Oct: at 30 spikes/s, the weakest tested input at which the real brain's turn command appeared, we also ran 2 scrambled brains per side (each neuron keeps its number of connections; one 1-second trial each, with the same trial seed as the real-wiring body runs). On the left side, neither scrambled brain sent a turn command, and their steering signals were identical to having no brain, so in the body they walk like the no-brain fly (the real wiring turned it about 11 deg). On the right side, neither scrambled brain sent a turn command, and their steering signals were identical to having no brain, so in the body they walk like the no-brain fly (the real wiring turned it about 17 deg). As at 80 spikes/s, none of the scrambled brains we ran produced the turn; at 30 spikes/s we did not turn their activity up to match the real brain.

The scrambled brains were not matched for overall activity: they fired 48% (left) and 44% (right) as many spikes as the real brain at this strength. Most of those spikes were the stimulated input neurons themselves; outside the input, the scrambled brains fired about 1% as many spikes as the real brain.

These body runs turn the brain's firing rates into steering through our own hand-made mapping, so they show the brain result in degrees; they are not a separate test of the wiring. We ran scrambled brains at 30 and 80 spikes/s, not at 20, 40 or 60.

Re-checked 17 Oct in the same no-GPU browser: 1 of 6 ran (3 Oct: 3 of 6). 4 new entries, 2 of them with a wiring comparison of their own (both read "helps" by our fixed rules; one moves a simulated hexapod by less than 1 mm).

Scrambled brains at 30 spikes/s

Our own test · pre-registered · brain only (Shiu et al. LIF on FlyWire v783) · one model · not a real fly

What we did. Every LC4 and LPLC2 neuron of one eye fired at 30 spikes/s for 1 s, the weakest input at which the real brain's turn command appeared on 8 Oct (far-side DNa02 8 Hz for a shadow on the left eye, 12 Hz on the right). We ran two degree-preserving scrambled brains per side (shuffle seeds 4 and 5: each neuron keeps its number of connections), one 1-second trial each, with trial seed 2, the seed of the real-wiring body runs at 30 spikes/s. The readouts, the turn rule (far DNa02 more than 3 times near DNa02 plus 5 Hz), the escape rule and the drive maker are the ones of 5-10 Oct, unchanged.

Checks before reading (all passed). Code: the only change to the 9 Oct trial script is an option that sets the trial seed of a scrambled brain (before, every scrambled brain ran at trial seed 0). Drive maker: our 8 Oct drive maker, run on the saved 8 Oct brain files, reproduced the fingerprints of both real 30 spikes/s drives and of the no-brain drive. Environment: the 8 Oct real-brain trial at 30 spikes/s (left eye, seed 2) was repeated exactly: 10,073 whole-brain spikes, 379 active neurons, 4,807 input spikes and every descending-neuron rate. Graphs: each scrambled brain passed its property checks and was the same graph as its 80 spikes/s twin.

Our own test · pre-registered before the first body run (14 Oct 2026, 09:28 UTC) · simulated body (FlyGym) driven through our own mapping · one model · a simulation, not an animal

On 8 Oct the brain model's turn command appeared from about 30 spikes per second per looming neuron. On 14 Oct we walked the remaining brain drives of that series in the simulated body, so every drive that differs from the no-brain floor now has a body run. The sentences were chosen by rules fixed in advance; the two eyes read differently, so there is one sentence per eye.

Left eye (pre-registered sentence): In the body, the fly turned away from 30 spikes/s per looming neuron upward on this side, the strength at which the brain's turn command appeared; the turn was about 11 deg at 30 and about 37 deg at 80 (our mapping turns the brain's rates into steering).

Right eye (pre-registered sentence): At 30 spikes/s the turn was borderline: the fly turned away in 1 of 2 runs on this side; from 40 upward it turned away in every run. This borderline rests on one run: seed 0 at 30 spikes/s turned 9.18° beyond the no-brain floor, 0.8° under our 10° rule (seed 2 at 30 turned 16.8°).

The relays: a stricter control, counted

The relay headline of 13 Oct stays: The two sides disagree: on one side silencing the relays lowered the turn neuron's rate more than matched activity elsewhere, on the other it did not. We do not draw a conclusion.

We also looked for a stricter control: other active inputs that excite the turn neuron with a similar firing rate and a similar number of connections onto it. In this model there are none for any of the active relay neurons on either side (count of 15 Oct: the busiest relay, PVLP141, fires about 100 spikes/s; no other such input fires above 43), so that stricter test cannot be built here.

This is a count from the map and the 15 Oct trial files, not a new trial. The relay panel with both charts: Is it real?

4 new entries and 2 new ledger rows

Project claims are the projects' own; ledger numbers are the authors', scored by our fixed rules and not recomputed by us.

  • Flycraft A (thepuneeth/Flycraft): a frozen MaleCNS v1.0 nerve-cord graph (11,000 nodes) is simulated as leaky integrate-and-fire neurons, and its motor-neuron rates set the joints of an 18-joint MuJoCo hexapod through a fixed hand-made map; there is no rhythm generator in the files read. In the author's test the real nerve-cord graph moved the hexapod 0.85 mm forward in 8 s, fully rewired graphs 3.5 mm backward, and a time-shuffled real signal 0.72 mm (shuffled weights 2.8 mm backward, zero drive 3.3 mm backward; the author's p for real vs time-shuffled is 0.29). The author's results file is marked pending the author's own review; the paper's acceptance is the author's statement only. Ledger: helps (fair, strong). The grade describes what the code does, not the size of the effect.
  • fly-connectome-reservoir B (cssius111): a fly-swatter game in which a frozen MaleCNS-derived network drives escape through a hand-designed decoder, with no wiring control on the game. Its separate reservoir test scores real wiring 100%, target-permuted wiring 83.9% and no connections 50% on a left/right task (3 seeds, trained linear readout); a raw-stimulus readout also reaches 100%. Ledger: helps (partly fair, strong); the no-brain baseline is not beaten.
  • virtual-fly-brain D (bookseal): a PPO policy steers FlyGym's own turning controller toward food; no connectome is in the control loop. Its own run report says the learning improvement was not shown.
  • Fruit Fly Connectome Explorer (n/a; chamodiHsenevirathna): ranks MaleCNS v1.0 neurons by connections and strength across weight thresholds; no neural dynamics.

Excluded on 17 Oct 2026 (no committed result file or wiring comparison in code at their current commit): wenlibo90/jumper-connectome, heyziq05/VirFly, arthen97/drawfly, snownmouse/cyber-fly-brain, undoaundo-ctrl/fly-brain-racing, Paradoxsmile/flybrain, Pratt2321/drosophila-carr-vision, marginli/brain_division and AloyMAitraz/fly-brain-visualizer.

The ledger now has 60 studies, 51 with a wiring null: helps 24, no difference 11, worse 4, mixed 10, not yet scored 2 (Does fly wiring help?).

What we could not resolve

  • Activity. The scrambled brains at 30 spikes/s were much quieter than the real brain outside the input (about 1%), and we did not turn them up. A turned-up arm at 30 spikes/s would need its own pre-registration.
  • Fly Worker's "no activity". The on-page text we track may have been static labels after a page change; we did not check by eye.
  • fly67. The 404 may be temporary, or the site may have moved again; we did not check its repository.
  • Flycraft. It meets our grade A rule as written, but its controlled effect is under 1 mm; whether the ledger needs a note for very small effects is open.
  • Leads. 31 new repositories wait for their first check.
