For AI agents

For AI agents: how to read, use and cite the fruit fly brain simulation catalogue

Short, stable instructions for agents and the people who build them. Everything here is plain HTML and static JSON files: the project catalogue, the video list and the controls ledger, each with a JSON Schema. There is no API.

  • Records: 104 projects, 52 videos, 33 control studies
  • Last verification: 2026-10-02
  • Catalogue version: 2026-10-02-run6 (projects and controls ledger)

Rules in brief

  1. Get the whole catalogue from projects.json with a plain HTTP GET, or read the Projects catalogue table. Each table row has the anchor #p-<id>.
  2. Use evidence_grade with mechanism and grade_basis to separate tested implementations from promotional claims. Never describe a grade C, D or U project as "driven by the fly brain". U means "claim only".
  3. For freshness, compare last_commit.date and last_release (upstream activity) with last_verified (our check). Ignore created_at for freshness.
  4. try_status "page responds, not played by us" means the page answered on that date. It does not mean the demo works.
  5. Numbers in measured_result were measured by the project's authors unless the text says we reproduced them. Never present them as independently verified.
  6. Cite the upstream source first, then our record with its last_verified date. Pass on data licences: FlyWire FAFB data is CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial).
  7. For "how do I build one", send users to Build your own and keep its tested and untested labels. The same applies to our project pages: the run steps for DOOMFLY and DesktopFly are all untested by us.
  8. When a record has api_fields_reused, its stars, created_at and pushed_at come from the earlier check dated api_fields_reused_from, not from last_verified. Say so if you quote them.
  9. For videos, use videos.json or the Video gallery (card anchors #v-<video_id>). A video's grade is its catalogue entry's grade: look it up by catalogue_id in projects.json, which is authoritative. Never grade a project from its video, and do not attach a grade to a video whose catalogue_id is null.
  10. Send users to downloads only through a record's code_url or try_url. We found copies under other GitHub accounts that reuse the names of catalogued projects (DesktopFly, FLM, FlyDrones) but only ship a zip file with a Windows program; we never list them, and you should not suggest them. DesktopFly has no official app download. The same applies to repositories that copy FlyGym's name: use the FlyGym record's code_url only. One FlyGym derivative with its own work is a catalogue entry of its own (flygym-banc-bridge, 2 Oct 2026); use its record's code_url.
  11. For "does the real fly wiring matter?", use controls and wiring_effect in projects.json, or the full ledger controls.json. wiring_effect "helps" means the real wiring beat a scrambled or rewired copy in that study, by the authors' numbers; it does not mean the project's behaviour is brain-driven, and it says nothing about a no-brain baseline (see baseline_beaten in the ledger). Quote the ledger's uncertainty with any count: fairness and quality labels are rule-based, and no study is replicated.
  12. Our own study, byo-shiu-shuffle, is a pre-registered test with four nulls (1 Oct 2026). Its wiring_effect is "mixed" by the fixed rules because, on its one metric (the MN9 rate), the sign shuffle's runaway counts as the null doing better. Do not report it as "the wiring does not matter": quote the arms (degree-preserving and weight shuffles 0 Hz against 85.2 Hz, boundary-preserving 39 Hz, sign shuffle 214 Hz with the whole brain ignited) and the limits (one reflex, one model, one trial per shuffle, gain check not matched on 3 shuffles: ×2.5 matched shuffle 3 only, and MN9 stayed at 0 Hz at every gain).
  13. Our second study, byo-flygym-body (2 Oct 2026), is a pre-registered open-loop body test: the Shiu et al. brain model drives FlyGym's walking fly through our hand-made rate-to-drive mapping. It is "no-difference", fair and strong by the fixed rules, but its 3 shuffles sent identical drives, so the scrambled arm is in effect one walk. Do not report it as "the fly brain does nothing": the real wiring added turning commands that scrambled wiring did not, and our mapping capped the drive, so forward distance could hardly differ. Quote it as: "in our test the FlyGym fly walked 13.9 mm with the real brain, 14.1 mm scrambled and 14.3 mm with no brain (one stimulus, one model, one body, 1 s, open loop)".
  14. The arm fields activity_ratio and activity_class (2 Oct 2026) are display only. They never enter wiring_effect, fairness or quality, and no scoring rule changed when they were added. Most arms are "not-reported".

projects.json has the top-level keys schema_version, catalogue, version, publisher, last_verification, record_count, schema and projects (the array of records). The Shaduf platform also publishes an Atom feed of site changes, linked from the head of every page.

Record fields

Field names are stable; new fields may be added. The full definitions are in projects.schema.json. Schema change on 28 Sep 2026: eight optional fields were added (listed below with that date); no field was removed or renamed, and enumerated values are unchanged, so older readers keep working. 29 Sep 2026: no schema change. Every record now carries an allowed value in platform, gpu and peer_review from the source, with any extra wording in the *_note fields; the published values are the same as before. 30 Sep 2026: two optional fields added, controls and wiring_effect, set on every record; nothing removed or renamed, so older readers keep working. 1 and 2 Oct 2026: no change to projects.json's schema (the controls ledger gained two display-only arm fields on 2 Oct, see below).

id
Stable lowercase slug, never reused. Also the table anchor #p-<id>.
name, author_or_org, summary
Project name, author or lab, and one or two sentences in our own words.
type
One of: dataset, data-tool, model, body, demo-game, demo-desktop, demo-art, browser-demo, research, directory.
claim
{text, url}: a short quote of the project's own headline claim. A claim, not a finding.
dataset, release
Connectome used (FlyWire FAFB, MaleCNS, BANC, MANC, hemibrain, FANC, larval L1, several, other, none, unknown) and its version.
evidence_grade
A, B, C, D, U or n/a. Definitions below.
grade_note
Our note when a grade is borderline or was reviewed. The grade itself is unchanged.
mechanism
wiring, neuron_model, input_mapping, output_mapping (hand-made, learned or none, with detail), trained_parts, body, scripted_parts. Null for datasets.
grade_basis, grade_date
What we inspected (file and line, or URL), each with a reliability label: [direct] we read it; [fetch-summary] read through a summarising tool; [search-summary] seen only in a search result. The date of that inspection.
measured_result
Numbers from a control or benchmark, measured by the authors unless stated.
try_url, try_status, try_note
No-install link, our check of it, and access notes such as a required sign-in.
code_url, paper_url, peer_review
Source repository, paper, and peer-reviewed / preprint / none (does not change the grade).
code_licence, code_licence_source
From the LICENSE file itself: an SPDX identifier, "none found", "custom: …" or "n/a (no code repository)". GitHub's own guess is kept separately in code_licence_source.
data_licence
Licence of the connectome data used. FlyWire FAFB: CC BY-NC 4.0. MaleCNS, MANC, hemibrain, BANC: CC BY.
platform, gpu, download_size
Where it runs (browser, macOS, Windows, Linux, Colab, iOS, Android, other); GPU required, optional, none or not stated; stated download size.
platform_note, gpu_note, peer_review_note
Added 28 Sep 2026. Our full wording when the enumerated value needs detail, for example gpu "required" with gpu_note "required (NVIDIA CUDA for training)". Prefer the note when you quote requirements.
stars, stars_date, archived
GitHub stars on that date (popularity, not quality) and archive status.
link_status
{url, status, http_code, final_url, checked_at}. Status is ok, redirect, 4xx, 5xx, blocked or error. "blocked" means a bot check (common on journal sites); it is not "dead". An http_code of 429 means our fetcher was rate-limited (it happens on YouTube watch pages); since 2 Oct 2026 such links have status "blocked", not "4xx", and they are not broken. A 404 on a code repository (FlyDoom, on 1 and 2 Oct 2026) means it was removed or made private; we keep the record and its last catalogued commit.
api_fields_reused, api_fields_reused_from
Added 28 Sep 2026. Which GitHub API fields (stars, created_at, pushed_at) were copied from an earlier check instead of re-queried, and that check's date.
archived_source, added_in_run, project_page
Added 28 Sep 2026. Where the archive status was read; the research run that first added the record (absent for first-release records); the route of our project page, when one exists.
controls
Added 30 Sep 2026. One of wiring-null (the project compares its fly wiring with shuffled, rewired or random wiring), baseline-only (only with a no-brain or no-graph baseline) or none. On 30 Sep 2026: 22, 8 and 64 records; on 1 Oct 2026: 23, 8 and 69.
wiring_effect
Added 30 Sep 2026. One of helps, no-difference, worse, mixed or not-tested, copied from the project's study in controls.json. Always not-tested when controls is not wiring-null, and also for a wiring null whose results are not in yet.
sources, notes_limitations
Source URLs with reliability labels, and known limits in our words.

Video records

videos.json has the top-level keys schema_version, collection, publisher, checked_at, record_count, schema, rules and videos (the array of records). The full definitions are in videos.schema.json.

video_id, page_anchor
Stable slug, never reused, and the card route /p/digital-fly-catalog/gallery/#v-<video_id>.
title, display_title, title_note
The title or first line as published, the card heading (long captions shortened), and why they differ.
platform, url, file_url
youtube, x, tiktok or project-site; the original page; a direct MP4 link for project-hosted files. playback is always "link-only": nothing plays inside our pages and nothing is re-hosted.
creator, upload_status, original_of, reposted_as
Uploader as displayed. original means the project's author, lab or institution posted it; repost points to its original with original_of; commentary is a third-party explainer that lists the entries it discusses in covers.
publish_date, duration, format
Date as shown by the platform (null when the host shows none), length, and frame shape (vertical, horizontal, square or file). Since 28 Sep 2026 duration and duration_s may be null when a project file states no length; covers may also list the projects that a control-study video tests, and grade_status may give the reason a video is not in the catalogue.
shows, does_not_show, summary_basis
Our own summary, the caveat taken from our verdicts, and what the summary rests on. We did not watch every video frame by frame.
catalogue_id, evidence_grade, grade_status
The matching entry in projects.json and a copy of its grade at build time. The catalogue record is authoritative; a video never changes a grade. catalogue_id null means "not in catalogue, not graded".
verdict_anchor
The project's row on Is it real?, when we wrote a verdict.
views, views_date
View count snapshot and its date. Popularity, not quality; it goes stale.
link_status, last_checked
Our check of the link and the platform record.

Controls ledger

controls.json (new on 30 Sep 2026) has the top-level keys schema_version, dataset, version, generated_at, run_id, as_of, answer (the one-line and short answer, and counts by task family), null_types (16 kinds of null or control, each with keeps, destroys, pitfall and is_wiring_null), studies and chart_rows. The full definitions are in controls.schema.json; the page is Does fly wiring help?

id
The catalogue id of the project, so you can join the two files. Two studies are ours and have no catalogue record (made_by_us true): byo-shiu-shuffle, our pre-registered fair test on the Build your own model, whose arms list each null type with every per-shuffle value in the source text; and byo-flygym-body, our body test (2 Oct 2026), whose arms are a degree-preserving null and two no-brain drives.
task_family, loop
One of reflex-circuit, sensory-model, body-steering, game-play, ml-benchmark, reservoir, language-model, forecasting, graph-analysis, evolution; and closed-loop, open-loop or offline.
metric, real, floor, arms
What was measured, the real wiring's value, the no-network floor where one exists, and every comparison arm with its null_type, value, sample count, spread, source file and recomputed_by_us. Since 2 Oct 2026 each arm also has activity_ratio (whole-brain spikes or active units, null ÷ real; null when not reported) and activity_class (reduced below 0.5, comparable 0.5–2, elevated 2–10, runaway above 10, or not-reported). Display only: they are set after all rules have run and never change a score.
effect
retention (0 = the headline null does no better than no network, 1 = as well as the real wiring), retention_method (floor, ratio or none), baseline_retention (the no-brain baseline as a share of the fly network's result), difference, interval, p-value and a note.
wiring_effect, baseline_beaten
helps, no-difference, worse, mixed or not-tested; and whether the fly network beat its no-brain baseline (yes, no or not-tested).
fairness, quality
Yes/no fields read from the code (degrees kept, input-output boundary kept, tuned equally, number of null samples, baseline present; held-out test, reported uncertainty, results in files, peer review) and the rule-based verdict (fair, partly-fair, unclear, unfair) and grade (strong, moderate, weak), each with a reason.
extraction, commit, checked_at, sources
Where the numbers come from (files, paper or catalogue-text), the commit read, when, and the source URLs.

Every number is the study authors' unless made_by_us is true. recomputed_by_us means we recomputed it from the authors' committed files, not that we re-ran the study.

Evidence grades

The grade measures how well the project's own claim of fly-brain-driven behaviour is supported. It does not measure quality or popularity. We assign the lowest grade whose conditions all hold, and only from code, data loading, configuration, paper methods or a result we reproduced, never from README text or search snippets alone.

A tested
Named connectome release → neural dynamics → output compared with real-fly data or a proper control (shuffled or random wiring, no-graph baseline). A well-controlled negative result is also A.
B connectome, hand-made I/O
Loads a named release and simulates activity that drives the behaviour; a person chose which neurons map to pixels, keys or joints. Trained readouts allowed but flagged in mechanism.trained_parts. No control.
C partial or stand-in
A small subset with set or trained weights, a synthetic stand-in network, connectome activity that only switches scripted programmes, or a network that is not the main source of the behaviour.
D scripted
Behaviour from ordinary code, animation, unconstrained reinforcement learning or a language model; neurons are cosmetic.
U unverified
We could not inspect the mechanism: no code, code that does not match the claim, or only a video or post.
n/a infrastructure
Datasets, bodies, simulators and analysis tools; described by licence, maintenance and setup fields instead.

Date meanings

last_commit.date
Upstream activity: date of the newest commit on the default branch, read from a git clone.
pushed_at
Upstream: GitHub's last push to any branch. Can be newer than last_commit (flybody: pushed 2026-02-07, default-branch commit 2025-07-30).
last_release
Upstream: newest GitHub release (tag and date), or "none". Tags without a release are not counted.
created_at
Repository creation date. Not an update date. Some other directories show it as if it were one.
last_verified
Our check: the day we last fetched the record's links over HTTP. Not a statement that the project works.
grade_date
Our check: the day we inspected the code or paper for the grade.

How to cite

  1. Cite the upstream source first: the repository or paper (code_url, paper_url) and, for data, the dataset paper (for example Dorkenwald et al. 2024 for FlyWire, Berg et al. 2026 for MaleCNS).
  2. Then cite our record as secondary context, with its last_verified date.
  3. Give a grade together with its grade_date. Never present a claim or an author-measured number as independently verified. For FlyWire-derived data, pass on the CC BY-NC 4.0 non-commercial condition.
Upstream: DOOMFLY, nftechie, https://github.com/nftechie/DOOMFLY

What we do not provide

  • No API. No query or search endpoint, no MCP server, no WebMCP tools, no login and no way to submit or edit records. The catalogue is static files regenerated by our research runs. (Virtual Fly Brain, an entry in our catalogue, runs its own MCP server; it is not ours, and we have not tested it.)
