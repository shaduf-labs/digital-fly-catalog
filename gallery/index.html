Video gallery: fruit fly brain simulation demos you can watch, and what each one really shows

Short clips of a "fly brain" playing Doom, driving a car or walking in Minecraft reached tens of millions of people. Here are 44 of those videos and the lab films behind them. Each card tells you who posted it, whether it is the original upload, and what the video does not show, next to the project's evidence grade from our catalogue.

  • Videos: 44 (4 added on 28 Sep 2026)
  • Checked: 28 Sep 2026
  • Plays on: the original platform (links open in a new tab)
  • JSON copy: videos.json

Short answer

A real connectome simulation drives what you see in the DOOMFLY, Mario 64 (Fly64), Stonkfly, Flyhard, FlyOCR, BioReservoir, flydoom and Fly.exe videos and in Blendi's Minecraft mod (grades A and B in our catalogue). In each of them, people designed how the input reaches the neurons and how the output is read from them. Flyhard also trained all of its connection strengths, FlyOCR reads its answer through a trained decoder, and Blendi's mod has a reflex layer that moves the fly when the brain is quiet.

New on 28 Sep 2026: a control study answers the Doom clip. Re-run with DOOMFLY's own code, a brainless autopilot that turns, walks and shoots at constant rates survived about as long as the full fly brain (51.9 s against 53.3 s). What that does and does not show.

Two more viral videos do not hold up in the code. The Half-Life video (grade D) says it uses the MaleCNS connectome, but the released code builds its own synthetic network by default. The Minecraft farming video (FlyBridge, grade C) uses a small trained part of the wiring, and scripted code walks, aims and clicks.

The three most-watched clips are the ones we could check least. Beat Saber and the NeuroCraft Minecraft fly have no code we could inspect (grade U), and in Bad Apple the picture is mostly the input video forced onto neurons (grade C). Eon's "brain upload" fly walks with pre-trained controllers by Eon's own account, and its body code is not public (U).

The lab films show something else: the flybody and NeuroMechFly videos show fly bodies moved by trained or hand-written controllers, not by a connectome, and the FlyWire, MaleCNS, hemibrain, MANC and BANC films show wiring maps, not simulations.

Why the videos open on their own platform

This site cannot play YouTube, X, TikTok or Vimeo videos in the page: embedded frames here run in a strict sandbox, and in our test on 28 September 2026 every one of those players failed inside it. So each card links to the original post, and we do not copy or re-host anyone's video. How we tested this.

Game, art and toy demos posted by their authors after the male fly connectome (MaleCNS v1.0) came out, and one control study that re-tested the Doom demo. In ten of these sixteen clips a real connectome runs and drives what you see. Five do not show that: two have no code we could inspect, one mostly shows its own input, one (Minecraft farming) uses scripted walking and aiming around a small trained part of the wiring, and one (Half-Life) does not load a connectome at all.

How we chose and checked these videos

What we did

  • Checked twice on 28 September 2026. Every link answered both times; the second check came about five hours after the first, when we also added 4 videos. We took the title, uploader, date and length from the platform's own record.
  • Original uploads first. A post counts as original when it comes from the project's author, lab or institution. We kept five reposts and commentary videos because many people saw them; each links to its original.
  • Summaries in our own words, from each video's title, description or caption, the project's own pages, and the thumbnail. Each card's "How we checked" note says what its summary rests on.
  • Grades come from code, not from videos. A card shows the grade of its catalogue entry, which we set by inspecting the project's code, data or paper. A video never raises or lowers a grade.

Limits

  • We did not watch every video frame by frame. A summary can miss something that appears only in the video.
  • View counts are snapshots from 28 September 2026 and will go stale.
  • One video is not in our catalogue and not graded: Ro0oney's Minecraft video. The mod it promotes publishes no code, and its site shows a crypto token address. SWOT's FlyBridge video is now linked to its catalogue entry (grade C).
  • Some new project files have no stated length (2) or publication date (2) on their host; their cards say so. Project-hosted files answered with HTTP 206 (a partial download), which is normal for video files.
  • We searched YouTube again on 28 September and found no new relevant upload. X and TikTok cannot be searched without logging in, so we did not search them.
  • Thumbnails load from YouTube's and X's image servers when you open this page, so those servers see your visit. TikTok and file videos get our own drawn cards instead.
  • Left out: a FlyDrones drone clip whose original post we could not find, two viral clips from projects we could not identify, low-reach re-uploads, a members-only video and video files we could not load. The research report lists them with reasons.
